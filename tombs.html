TOMBS OF THE PROPHETS

XR Experience Pre-Plan

Concept: A WebXR pilgrimage to the burial/memorial site of every prophet across every tradition on earth, accessible to anyone with a phone and a $10 cardboard headset. The journey begins not on earth but in orbit, at the tomb of a prophet who hasn't been born yet.

THE OPENING

You float in low earth orbit. Below you, the planet. Around you, nothing but the hum of a station that doesn't exist yet.

In front of you: a tomb. Corten steel. Weathered. Oxidized like it's been here for centuries, which is impossible because you're in space. The tomb has no name. No dates. No inscription except a single glyph that your language doesn't have a character for.

This is Tomb Zero. The future prophet. Laid to rest off-planet because the earth they served was not the only earth.

You touch the tomb (tap the screen). The rust flakes. The camera pulls back. The earth fills your view. And then you fall toward it.

You land at your first earthbound tomb.

SCOPE: "EVERY PROPHET EVER KNOWN"

Canonical Traditions (Named Prophets)

Hebrew Bible / Tanakh (Nevi'im)

  • Major: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Daniel
  • Twelve Minor: Hosea, Joel, Amos, Obadiah, Jonah, Micah, Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, Haggai, Zechariah, Malachi
  • Pre-classical: Moses, Aaron, Miriam, Samuel, Elijah, Elisha, Nathan, Gad, Deborah
  • Patriarchs-as-prophets: Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph
  • ~48 prophets recognized in Talmudic tradition (Megillah 14a)

Islamic Tradition (Anbiya/Rusul)

  • 25 named in Quran: Adam, Idris (Enoch), Nuh (Noah), Hud, Salih, Ibrahim (Abraham), Lut (Lot), Ismail, Ishaq, Yaqub, Yusuf, Ayyub (Job), Shu'ayb, Musa (Moses), Harun (Aaron), Dhul-Kifl, Dawud (David), Sulayman (Solomon), Ilyas (Elijah), Alyasa (Elisha), Yunus (Jonah), Zakariyya, Yahya (John), Isa (Jesus), Muhammad
  • Hadith tradition: 124,000 prophets sent to humanity (we represent this as a field of unnamed markers, not 124,000 individual tombs)
  • Known tomb sites: Cave of the Patriarchs (Hebron), Prophet's Mosque (Medina), numerous maqams across Middle East

Christian Tradition

  • OT prophets (shared with Judaism)
  • John the Baptist (tomb venerated in Umayyad Mosque, Damascus)
  • Jesus (Church of the Holy Sepulchre / Garden Tomb, Jerusalem)
  • Some traditions: apostles as prophetic figures
  • Marian apparition sites (prophetic encounters, not prophets per se -- include as waypoints?)

Zoroastrian

  • Zarathustra/Zoroaster (no confirmed tomb, tradition places him in Balkh, Afghanistan)
  • Saoshyant (future savior figures -- three of them, prophesied)

Baha'i Faith

  • The Bab (Shrine of the Bab, Haifa)
  • Baha'u'llah (Shrine of Baha'u'llah, Acre)

Mandaean

  • Yahya (John the Baptist, shared with Islam/Christianity)

Druze

  • Jethro/Shu'ayb (Nabi Shu'ayb, near Tiberias)

Manichaeism

  • Mani (died in Gundeshapur, Iran)

Indigenous / Oral Traditions

  • This is where it gets ethically complex.
  • Some are not meant to be named publicly.
  • Some have no "tomb" concept.
  • Representation requires consultation, not extraction.
  • Placeholder: "The Unnamed" -- a section of the experience acknowledging prophetic traditions that cannot or should not be catalogued by outsiders.

Modern / Contested

  • Joseph Smith (LDS, Sharon, Vermont birthplace / Nauvoo, Illinois)
  • Others as research surfaces them

Total Estimated Named Sites: 80-120 unique locations. Represented-but-not-named traditions: dozens more.

NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

The experience is NOT a museum. It's a pilgrimage. The difference:

  • Museum: Here is information about this prophet.
  • Pilgrimage: You walked here. You stood here. Something shifted.

Journey Arc

TOMB ZERO (orbit) -- the unnamed future
    |
    v
DESCENT -- fall to earth, land at nearest prophet's tomb to user's GPS
    |
    v
THE WALK -- move between tombs by tradition or geography (user chooses)
    |
    v
CROSSINGS -- moments where traditions overlap at the same physical site
    (Hebron, Jerusalem, Damascus -- prophets shared across faiths)
    |
    v
THE UNNAMED -- a vast dark field of 124,000 lights
    (the hadith number, the uncountable prophets of all traditions)
    |
    v
RETURN TO ORBIT -- Tomb Zero again, but now you've walked the earth

Two Navigation Modes

Geographic: Walk the earth. Tombs appear where they physically are. You move between them like a pilgrim. Middle East is dense. Central Asia is sparse. The gaps are the experience.

Tradition: Follow one thread. All the Hebrew prophets. All the Quranic prophets. Watch how they overlap, diverge, reconverge.

AESTHETIC: SERRA IN SPACE

  • Every tomb rendered as Corten steel. Not marble. Not gold. Rust.
  • Richard Serra's vocabulary: massive curved planes, torqued ellipses, tilted arcs
  • The tombs are not decorative. They have WEIGHT even in zero-G.
  • Texture: photogrammetric rust, pitting, scale, patina
  • Lighting: sharp, directional, like Texas afternoon sun even in orbit
  • Sound: low frequency hum. Metal stress. Wind through gaps in steel.
  • No text floats in space. Information is revealed by proximity and touch.
  • The Corten weathers differently at each site -- desert tombs are dry-oxidized, coastal tombs show salt corrosion, the space tomb has a frost-burn patina that doesn't exist in nature

What This Is NOT

  • Not a glossy museum with floating info panels
  • Not gamified (no achievements, no collectibles)
  • Not educational-first (the learning is a consequence of the awe, not the other way around)

TECH STACK

Target Hardware

  • Floor: Google Cardboard / any phone-based VR holder
  • Ceiling: Quest 3, Vision Pro (progressive enhancement)
  • Middle: Phone AR without headset (hold-up-and-look mode)

Framework

Astro (static site generator) as the shell:

  • Pre-renders each tomb as its own page/route
  • Handles the content layer (prophet data, tradition metadata, site coordinates)
  • Static-first means it loads fast on bad connections (pilgrimage sites often have bad cell service -- ironic requirement but real)
  • Islands architecture: only the XR viewport is interactive JS

A-Frame (WebXR framework built on Three.js):

  • Battle-tested for Cardboard-tier WebVR
  • Declarative HTML-like components
  • Large ecosystem of community components
  • Works in mobile browser with WebXR polyfill
  • Handles the 3D rendering, spatial audio, interaction

Content Layer

  • Each prophet: JSON record with name, tradition(s), dates, coordinates, tomb description, shared-with references
  • GeoJSON for mapping mode
  • Astro content collections for the data

Assets

  • Corten steel materials: procedural shader (noise-based rust) rather than heavy textures
  • Keeps download size small for mobile
  • Each tomb geometry: parametric, generated from a seed value unique to the prophet
  • No two tombs look the same but they're all clearly from the same vocabulary
  • Serra's curves, but each one computed, not hand-modeled

Progressive Enhancement Tiers

| Tier | Device | Experience |
|------|--------|------------|
| 1 | Cardboard + phone | Stereoscopic 3D, head tracking, tap interaction |
| 2 | Phone AR (no headset) | Camera passthrough, tombs overlaid on real world |
| 3 | Quest / standalone | Full 6DOF, hand tracking, walk-around |
| 4 | Vision Pro | Spatial computing, mixed reality, highest fidelity |
| Fallback | Any browser | 2D interactive map with 3D tomb viewer embedded |

ETHICAL GUARDRAILS

  1. No tradition is presented as more valid than another. The Corten steel is the equalizer. Every prophet gets the same material vocabulary.
  2. Indigenous/oral traditions are acknowledged, not extracted. "The Unnamed" section exists specifically to honor what should not be catalogued without permission.
  3. Shared prophets are rendered ONCE at their physical site with crossing indicators showing which traditions claim them. The user experiences the overlap, not the argument.
  4. No tomb is "empty" or "false." If a tradition holds a site sacred, it's sacred in the experience. Historicity debates are not the point.
  5. The future prophet (Tomb Zero) belongs to no tradition. It's a question, not an answer.
  6. Consultation required before launch: Reach out to scholars/practitioners in each tradition for accuracy review. Budget time and money for this. It's not optional.

  • Astro + A-Frame scaffold
  • One tomb: Tomb Zero in orbit
  • Corten steel procedural shader
  • Cardboard-compatible WebXR
  • Test on your phone with a $10 headset
  • Deliverable: you float in space, you see the tomb, you touch it, you fall

Phase 1: Earth Landing (3-4 weeks)

  • 5 tombs: Abraham (Hebron), Moses (Mt. Nebo), Muhammad (Medina), Jesus (Jerusalem), Zarathustra (Balkh)
  • Geographic navigation between them
  • Descent animation from orbit
  • Fallback 2D map view
  • Content collection structure in Astro

Phase 2: Full Abrahamic (6-8 weeks)

  • All Hebrew, Islamic, Christian named prophets
  • Crossing system for shared sites
  • Tradition-based navigation mode
  • Spatial audio (wind, metal stress, site-specific ambience)
  • Progressive enhancement for Quest/Vision Pro

Phase 3: Beyond Abrahamic + The Unnamed (4-6 weeks)

  • Zoroastrian, Baha'i, Mandaean, Druze, Manichaean
  • "The Unnamed" -- 124,000 lights field
  • Ethical review with tradition consultants
  • Performance optimization for the full dataset

Phase 4: Polish + Ship

  • Loading experience (the fall from orbit as loading screen)
  • Offline mode (progressive web app for actual pilgrimage use)
  • Accessibility (screen reader mode for 2D fallback, audio descriptions)
  • Documentation

OPEN QUESTIONS

  1. Tomb Zero glyph: What is it? A character from no existing script? A procedurally generated symbol? Something from your Hebrew studies that points forward?
  2. The 124,000: Render as particle field? As sound? As a constellation viewed from orbit before descent? All three?
  3. User's own tradition: Does the experience ask? Does it affect the journey order? Or is everyone equal -- no personalization, just pilgrimage?
  4. Serra estate: The aesthetic is inspired by, not replicating specific works. But worth noting the reference openly in credits rather than dancing around it.
  5. Monetization: Free? Donation-based? Grant-funded? This is the kind of project NEA/NEH digital humanities grants exist for. Also fits Sundance New Frontier.
  6. Name: Working title "Tombs of the Prophets" is descriptive. But it could be sharper. Hebrew: "Kivrei HaNevi'im" (קברי הנביאים) -- which is also literally a site in Jerusalem (the ancient burial caves on the Mount of Olives). That collision is interesting.

WHAT THIS IS REALLY ABOUT

Everyone argues about whose prophet is real. Whose book is right. Whose tomb is holy.

You put them all in the same rust. Same weight. Same material. Same gravity.

And you start the whole thing with someone who doesn't exist yet. In a place no one's been buried.

The argument dissolves in the oxidation.
